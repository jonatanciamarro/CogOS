# FOUNDATIONS-0001: Epistemology of Cognitive Ecosystems

## 1. Scope
To define the nature of meaning, signals, belief, knowledge, and evidence within Cognitive Systems Engineering (CSE), establishing a clear line between raw information and verified cognitive state.

---

## 2. The Epistemic Hierarchy

Information is not homogeneous; it exists in distinct, structured epistemic states:

1. **Signal**: Raw, unstructured digital or environmental data. A signal is a state transition recorded by a sensor or received from an external process.
2. **Belief**: A representation or hypothesis held by a cognitive organism or cortex. A belief has internal structure but lack complete external verification.
3. **Evidence**: Evaluated, context-grounded signals that support or refute a specific belief, measured against a trusted observer baseline.
4. **Knowledge**: A belief that has been verified through rigorous proof or empirical evidence, reconciled against known counterexamples, and contextualized within the system ontology.
5. **Meaning**: The relational mapping of how a signal, belief, or piece of knowledge changes the internal state, world model, or decision space of an observer.

---

## 3. Epistemic Transition Equations

Epistemic states are transformed through formal cognitive processes:

* **Perception**: 
  $$\text{Signal} \xrightarrow{\text{Context}} \text{Interpretation (Belief)}$$
* **Verification**: 
  $$\text{Belief} + \text{Evidence} \xrightarrow{\text{Validation}} \text{Knowledge}$$
* **Decay**: 
  $$\text{Knowledge} \xrightarrow{\text{Time} \times \text{Entropy}} \text{Belief}$$

---

## 4. Grounding Axioms

* **Observer-Dependency**: Meaning cannot exist in isolation; it is defined strictly relative to the observer interpreting it.
* **Dynamic Epistemic Weight**: Knowledge is held conditionally. The epistemic status of any concept or primitive fluctuates dynamically based on the current evidence matrix.
