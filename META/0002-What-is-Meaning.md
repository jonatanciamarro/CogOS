# META-0002: What is Meaning?

## 1. The Question
How is "meaning" generated, conserved, and transferred within a cognitive ecosystem?

---

## 2. Competing Theories

### Theory A: Representational Semantic Theory (Static)
* **Definition**: Meaning is a static correspondence mapping between a symbol (or token) and an objective real-world referent or predefined conceptual schema.
* **Assumes**: Meaning resides within the representation or symbol itself.
* **Predicts**: Establishing a unified database schema or universal ontology maps all meanings without loss or ambiguity.
* **Testable By**: Translating concepts across different schema maps and measuring information loss.

### Theory B: Relational Transition Theory (Dynamic)
* **Definition**: Meaning is a dynamic operator. It is the trajectory of state transitions triggered in an observer when exposed to a signal under a specific context.
* **Assumes**: Meaning is relational, observer-dependent, and non-local. It does not reside in the signal, but in the delta: 
  $$\text{Meaning} = f(\text{Signal}, \text{Observer State}, \text{Context}) \to \Delta \text{Observer State}$$
* **Predicts**: The exact same signal will carry different meanings—and trigger different actions—if either the context or the observer's state history is altered.
* **Testable By**: Sending an identical signal to two nodes with divergent memory states and measuring the divergence of their decision vectors.

---

## 3. Evidence Matrix

| Phenomenon / Anomaly | Theory A (Representational) | Theory B (Relational) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Context-dependency (Shifting interpretation)** | Poor (requires infinite nested definitions) | Strong (context is a native term of the transition equation) |
| **Heterogeneous systems integration** | Moderate (requires manual schema translation mapping) | Strong (focuses on equivalence of state changes, not exact token alignment) |
| **Semantic drift over time** | Poor (labels drift as errors) | Strong (drift is a natural result of observer history updates) |

---

## 4. Current Best Theory
**Theory B (Relational Transition Theory)** is selected as the current best theory.

* **Justification**: In organizational environments, a static dictionary approach collapses because language models and human teams interpret expressions differently depending on current issues and goals. By defining meaning as the *actual impact* a signal makes on an observer's internal model, we can measure and design systems that preserve functional coordination even when using different vocabularies.
