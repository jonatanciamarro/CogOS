# META-0004: What is Intelligence?

## 1. The Question
What is the definition and the smallest irreducible unit of intelligence within Cognitive Systems Engineering?

---

## 2. Competing Theories

### Theory A: Problem-Solving Agency (Goal-Oriented Optimization)
* **Definition**: Intelligence is the capacity of an agent to optimize behavior and select actions that maximize the probability of achieving specific, pre-determined goals in complex environments.
* **Assumes**: Intelligence is goal-driven, agent-centric, and measurable via task optimization.
* **Predicts**: System intelligence is proportional to performance on static task benchmarks (e.g., standardized tests, game play).
* **Testable By**: Measuring task success rates across fixed benchmarks.

### Theory B: Epistemic Adaptation & Compression (Evolutionary)
* **Definition**: Intelligence is the rate at which a cognitive ecosystem compresses experience, updates its internal world model, and restructures its processes to adapt to environmental changes.
* **Assumes**: The fundamental unit of intelligence is the **Cognitive Transformation**—the loop of translating signals into belief updates to increase semantic integrity and reduce entropy.
* **Predicts**: A highly intelligent system can adapt to sudden environmental rule changes by modifying its internal ontologies, whereas a system based on optimization will collapse when its target metrics are disrupted.
* **Testable By**: Introducing unexpected perturbations into a simulated system's task rules and measuring the time required to re-establish stable outputs.

---

## 3. Evidence Matrix

| Phenomenon / Anomaly | Theory A (Agency) | Theory B (Adaptation) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Solving static chess/go games** | Strong (agents optimize action selection) | Moderate (interprets play as execution of a compressed state model) |
| **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) collapse** | Poor (cannot explain why optimizing agents fail on minor changes to input rules) | Strong (attributes failure to rigid world models and lack of real-time transformation) |
| **Organizational intelligence** | Poor (struggles to model emergent intelligence using simple agent-goal frameworks) | Strong (models organizational learning as collective ontology updates under epistemic pressure) |

---

## 4. Current Best Theory
**Theory B (Epistemic Adaptation & Compression)** is selected as the current best theory.

* **Justification**: In CSE, we do not design static goal-seekers. We design ecosystems that must survive changes in models, technologies, and human environments. Intelligence is the velocity at which the system updates its internal structures in response to new observations. Its irreducible unit is the transition from raw signal to evolutionary learning.
