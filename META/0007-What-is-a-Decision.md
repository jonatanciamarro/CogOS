# META-0007: What is a Decision?

## 1. The Question
What constitutes a "decision" in a cognitive ecosystem, and how is it executed?

---

## 2. Competing Theories

### Theory A: Expected Utility Optimization (Rationalist)
* **Definition**: A decision is the selection of an action from a set of alternatives that maximizes a calculated expected utility function under known probability distributions.
* **Assumes**: Action pathways, outcomes, and values are fully parameterizable. The system's goal is utility optimization.
* **Predicts**: Decisions will always converge on the mathematical optimum given the available information.
* **Testable By**: Testing decision loops in static simulated environments with explicit mathematical reward matrices.

### Theory B: Constraint Resolution & Epistemic Collapse (Dynamic)
* **Definition**: A decision is the collapse of multiple competing belief states and possibilities into a single, coordinated action pathway, triggered to resolve epistemic pressure and satisfy internal invariants.
* **Assumes**: Absolute utility is non-computable under systemic uncertainty. The system acts to preserve its semantic integrity.
* **Predicts**: Nodes will prioritize choices that stabilize their internal world model and satisfy core rules over paths offering high theoretical yield but high semantic risk.
* **Testable By**: Creating a scenario where a high-yield path introduces severe ontological contradictions, and observing if the node rejects it in favor of a lower-yield, stable path.

---

## 3. Evidence Matrix

| Phenomenon / Anomaly | Theory A (Utility) | Theory B (Constraint Resolution) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Solving structured math/chess** | Strong (utility maximizing paths are clear) | Moderate (models execution as constraint satisfaction of rules) |
| **Bounded rationality in action** | Poor (requires patching the model with ad-hoc heuristics and error variables) | Strong (bounded rationality is a native property of constraint satisfaction under time limits) |
| **Commitment bias / Legacy persistence** | Poor (implies persistent calculation errors by the agent) | Strong (system protects its core ontology map from collapsing, even at financial cost) |

---

## 4. Current Best Theory
**Theory B (Constraint Resolution & Epistemic Collapse)** is selected as the current best theory.

* **Justification**: Cognitive systems operating in unpredictable environments cannot rely on global utility equations; they must act to survive and maintain structural stability. A decision is the boundary transition where the system collapses probabilistic reasoning into a singular commitment of resources, resolving internal tension so the ecosystem can continue to act and learn.
